The Rotation Lattice

Forty synthetic interviews went through the CoForge pipeline end to end. The corpus yields one genuinely useful finding, and it is not about travellers. It is that the most obvious analysis of this data would have been confident, well-presented, and entirely an artefact of how the data was generated.

What the corpus actually is

It is not a sample. It is a lattice. Each segment of ten participants is confined to exactly four pain themes, and within that segment the theme pairs cycle through a fixed rotation: participant one gets themes one and two, participant two gets two and three, participant three gets three and four, participant four gets four and one, then it repeats. The same pattern runs in all four segments, offset by four themes each time.

Checking the analysis that came with it

The corpus arrived with its own findings document. It is honest in important ways: it labels itself synthetic throughout, it says guide performance is the real purpose, and it warns that saturation may be model convergence. Those hold up. Five other claims do not.

The instrument was tested at two levels, not three

Participants carry a declared verbosity: terse, average, or talkative. The findings document reports that terse participants gave short answers and the guide coped. It did cope. But terse and average are indistinguishable in the corpus.

What the pipeline caught that reading would not

This run was aimed at the machinery rather than the market. Three things surfaced only because the corpus was pushed through the gates rather than summarised.

  • The artifact type collides with its own subject. CoForge registers interview-analysis as “cited to the ledger”, and its checklist requires every quote to be present there. ADR-024 forbids exactly that for a synthetic corpus. The contract assumed real research, and nothing had ever tested it against generated participants.
  • Layer 1 refused the corpus, correctly. Writing the three files into research/sources/ was denied. That path is deny-listed so an agent cannot manufacture evidence, and a shell copy is the documented way around it. The corpus is recorded here by SHA-256 and stays outside the repository until a human admits it.
  • The registered chart palette is unusable. cf-chart-palette is deprecated and defective: its four series sit on one lightness step and measure 1.055:1 against each other. The palette on this page was built from CoForge ramps at four different steps and validated by measurement, in both themes, before any chart was drawn.

The guide asks people to design the product

Every one of the forty interviews ends the same way: if you could change one thing, what would it be? It is asked in all 40, in three segment-specific wordings. It is a reasonable closing question and it is also a solution-eliciting one — it invites a feature request rather than a description of a problem.

That matters because of where it lands. The roster's “voiced need / opportunity” column — the closest thing this corpus has to a product requirement — is downstream of that answer for at least 21 of the 40 participants on a conservative match. So the opportunity list is substantially a transcription of what people said when asked to name a fix, not what they struggled with when asked to tell a story.

What to change

Three groups, in the order they pay off. Everything here is drawn from the corpus rather than from the market.

The guide, before you field it with anyone

  • Stop ending on a solution. Keep the question if you like it, but ask a problem-side question after it, and stop treating the answers as demand. A wish named in the last minute of an interview is the weakest signal in the transcript, and here it is the one that became the opportunity list.
  • Add a return-and-after probe. One interview in forty touches the stage. For business travellers the whole expenses pain lives there, and it is currently reached sideways through a cost question rather than directly.
  • Give the four guides a shared spine. Exactly one question is asked of all 40. Each segment has about eight core questions of its own. That makes cross-segment comparison nearly impossible — and it is the second reason, after the lattice, that the “cuts across every segment” claim could not have been supported.
  • Test a repair that fails. Seven curveball behaviours, seventeen instances, and every single one resolves on the moderator's first attempt. Real participants do not. The guide has never been tested against a second refusal.
  • Fix or drop the terse band. Terse and average differ by 1.5 words. Either make terse genuinely terse or admit there are two bands, because right now one third of the roster is testing nothing.

The generator, if there is another synthetic round

  • Let themes cross segments. Sample them, do not rotate them. Until a theme can appear anywhere, no frequency and no segment difference can ever mean anything, and every such finding will be circular in exactly the way this one was.
  • Vary how many pains a participant has. Exactly two each is what makes the totals arithmetic.
  • Define what counts as an access need and count it once. The corpus states eleven; the review says eight; nothing says which definition is in use.

CoForge itself

  • Sign or reject ADR-024. This artifact depends on it and it is still proposed.
  • Give the interview-analysis checklist a synthetic branch, or rule that the type does not accept synthetic corpora. Today it demands ledger citations that ADR-024 forbids.
  • Build the check ADR-024 admits it is owed: an [E-nnn] citation inside an artifact declaring corpus: synthetic should be a blocker. It is prose until then, which is the weakest layer.
  • Promote the validated palette through the membrane to replace the defective cf-chart-palette. It exists and is measured; it is not yet in the index.

What this run does not establish

Everything above describes a corpus and a pipeline. None of it describes a traveller. The competitor benchmark already records the first-timer premise as unresearched and carried at low confidence, and these interviews were generated under that premise. Agreement between them is not corroboration; it is the premise meeting itself.

The guide-facing questions this run raises — which stages stay thin, whether the repair moves hold, what a genuinely terse participant does to a 45-minute script — are answerable only by fielding it with people.
