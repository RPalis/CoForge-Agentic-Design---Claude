# Verified Competitor Profile · Booking.com

**Phase:** 2 of 9, competitor 2 of 14
**Date researched:** 21 July 2026
**Researcher session conditions:** booking.com, signed out, desktop web, browser located in Spain, currency EUR, English language. Analytical and marketing cookies were declined at the consent banner. Region, language and account state affect what is on screen, so every observation below is conditional on this state.
**Platform covered:** desktop web only. The mobile apps were not opened.
**Evidence supplied by the user:** none. Public sources only.

No comparison to any other product appears in this document. That is Phase 3's job.

---

## 1. Snapshot

Booking.com is a transactional travel platform selling six verticals from one header: Stays, Flights, Flight + Hotel, Car rental, Attractions, and Airport taxis (High, live product). It states that it does not buy or resell products and is paid commission by service providers after a booking, with an additional net-rate-plus-markup model in car rental (Medium, first-party How we work page). A named badge programme, Preferred Partner, is disclosed as indicating a property that pays higher commission, and paid placements carry an "Ad" label (Medium, same page). The loyalty programme, Genius, is tenure based: Level 1 unlocks on creating an account, Level 2 after 5 bookings in 2 years, Level 3 after 15 bookings in 2 years (Medium, first-party Genius page). The corporate entity differs per vertical, with accommodation and flights under Booking.com B.V. in the Netherlands and car rental and ground transport under Booking.com Transport Limited in the United Kingdom (Medium, How we work). The platform operates across desktop web, mobile web and apps, and states it serves users in the European Economic Area with a documented personalised-recommendation opt-out (Medium). The full market list was not established this session (Unknown).

## 3. UX patterns

**Navigation model.** A blue header carries the six verticals as a persistent tab row, with an account icon and a menu control on the right. On content pages the header also exposes currency, country, a help icon, List your property, Register and Sign in. Search state is carried in the URL, including sort and filter parameters. A breadcrumb trail (Home, Portugal, Lisbon, Search results) sits above stays results. (High, live product.)

**Onboarding and first run.** No account or preference capture precedes search. The consent banner is the first interaction and offers Decline, Accept and Manage settings as three distinct controls. Two optional context checkboxes sit under the homepage search bar, "I'm traveling for work" and "Add flights to my search", so trip context can be declared before searching rather than filtered afterwards. Sign-in is prompted through benefit framing on the homepage and inside results, and the auth wall sits at the trips surface. (High, live product.)

**Primary flow, search to selection.** Enter destination and dates, land on results with a labelled default sort, refine with a long left-hand filter rail where every option carries a live result count, open a property, then select a rate from a table. Result counts appear at every level: the headline count, per-filter counts, review counts, and the count of reviews behind a sub-score. (High, live product.)

**Ranking transparency.** The disclosure is placed as a banner directly above the first result rather than as a link beside the sort control, and it names commission explicitly in the banner text itself. Behind it, a first-party page sets out per-vertical ranking factors in detail: for accommodation, click-through rate, gross bookings and net bookings as the primary three, with commission level, payment speed, Genius and Preferred Partner participation, and in the United States whether Booking.com organises payment, named as further influences. The page also states that the default-ranking factors persist as tiebreakers under any chosen sort. Badge semantics are documented: a thumbs-up badge denotes the Preferred Partner Program and higher commission, and "Ad" denotes paid placement. (High for the banner, Medium for the page.)

**Personalisation and its controls.** The default sort adapts to declared occupancy, labelled "Top Picks for Solo Travelers" on a one-adult search. Property content adapts in the same direction, with a solo-specific location score and a "Popular with solo travelers" highlight. The first-party page documents an opt-out for users in the EEA, reachable through a "Manage personalized recommendations" footer link, and states that when signed out the preference is stored in a cookie and expires with it. (High for the interface, Medium for the opt-out mechanics.)

**Natural-language input.** Smart filters accept a free-text description and convert it into standard, removable filter chips rather than into an opaque result set. The conversion is visible and reversible: the user can see which filters were inferred and remove any of them. In the tested case, two of three requested attributes were converted and the third, a language requirement, produced no chip. (High, live product.)

**Reassurance mechanics.** Reassurance appears as plain-text rate terms rather than as a single named guarantee: "Free cancellation before [date]", "Pay nothing until [date]", "No prepayment needed, pay at the property", "You won't be charged yet", "Includes taxes and fees". Trust framing is separate and appears as counted evidence: review counts, the number of reviews behind a sub-score, "Real guests, Real stays, Real opinions", verified host-type labelling, and a licence number in the fine print. Documented recovery policies exist for overbooking and for mispricing. (High for the on-page instances, Medium for the policies.)

**Accessibility as filterable inventory.** Eighteen accessibility attributes are exposed as ordinary filters in the results rail, split into Property Accessibility and Room Accessibility, each with a result count. Property pages list "Facilities for disabled guests" among most popular facilities. Separately, a published accessibility statement describes the platform's own conformance work. (High for the filters, Medium for the statement.)

**Errors and empty states.** No zero-results state was reached. Two disclosure states were observed in place of conventional empty states: a banner stating what proportion of stock is unavailable under the current filters, and dismissible informational banners above results. Unresolvable content URLs redirected to the homepage rather than returning an error page, with the attempted path preserved in a tracking label. (High, live product.)

**Notifications.** Flight price alerts are documented as a Genius Level 1 benefit delivered in the app. No notification or alert control was observed on the desktop web surfaces walked. Notification content and cadence are `Unknown`. (Medium for the documented benefit.)

**Feedback capture.** Satisfaction questions are embedded inside page content rather than in a separate survey surface, and appear on multiple page types. Content-completeness prompts ("Missing some information? Yes / No") sit next to individual information blocks. (High, live product.)

## 4. Notable design decisions

These look deliberate and are stated without evaluation.

1. **The commission disclosure is a banner above the results, not a link.** It appears in the reading path before the first property, states commission by name, and can be dismissed.
2. **Every filter option carries a live result count.** The consequence of applying a filter is legible before it is applied, across all filter groups.
3. **Accessibility is treated as filterable inventory, not as a policy page.** Eighteen attributes across two groups, each counted, sit in the same rail as budget and breakfast.
4. **Natural-language input is converted into visible, removable structured filters.** The system's interpretation is shown as chips the user can edit, rather than applied invisibly.
5. **The product discloses the limits of its own inventory in a filtered state.** After filtering, a banner stated what percentage of places to stay are unavailable on the site.
6. **The default sort is named after the traveller type inferred from occupancy.** A one-adult search produced "Top Picks for Solo Travelers", and two traveller-type sorts are offered as explicit options.
7. **Review evidence is segmented by traveller type.** The property page reported a location score specific to one-person stays alongside the overall score.
8. **Sub-scores are published with the sample size behind them.** The location score was presented as "rated 9.1/10, score from 3720 reviews".
9. **Loyalty is documented with numeric thresholds on a public page.** Level 2 at 5 bookings in 2 years and Level 3 at 15 bookings in 2 years are stated, and priority support is placed at Level 3.
10. **Two loyalty statements sit side by side without reconciliation.** The page states rewards are "yours to enjoy for life" once unlocked, and the same page carries the FAQ "Why is my Genius level lower than it was before?".
11. **Trip context is declared before search, not filtered after.** "I'm traveling for work" and "Add flights to my search" are checkboxes beneath the homepage search bar.
12. **Discovery is offered on a non-price axis.** The homepage trip planner asks the user to pick a theme, then returns destinations by distance, rather than by price.
13. **Badge semantics are published.** The thumbs-up badge is documented as the Preferred Partner Program with higher commission, and quality ratings are documented as machine-learning outputs across 400 or more features while star ratings are documented as not assigned by Booking.com.
14. **Recovery policies are written with specific remedies.** The overbooking section states free cancellation, assistance finding a similar alternative, and refund of the price difference on submission of the alternative invoice.
15. **Public transit is listed on the property page as named stops with distances**, alongside attractions and airports.
16. **Question answering is offered before it is asked.** Ten preset questions and a further twenty are shown, with free-text asking positioned as the fallback and captioned "We have an instant answer to most questions".
17. **Consent offers Decline as a primary control.** Decline, Accept and Manage settings are presented as three separate controls in the first-run banner.
