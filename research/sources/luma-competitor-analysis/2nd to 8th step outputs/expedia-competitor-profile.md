# Verified Competitor Profile · Expedia

**Phase:** 2 of 9, competitor 1 of 14
**Date researched:** 21 July 2026
**Researcher session conditions:** expedia.com (US site), signed out, desktop web, browser located in Spain, currency USD. A banner offered the Spain site at Expedia.es. Region and account state affect what is on screen, so every observation below is conditional on this state.
**Platform covered:** desktop web only. The mobile apps were not opened.
**Evidence supplied by the user:** none. Public sources only.

No comparison to any other product appears in this document. That is Phase 3's job.

---

## 1. Snapshot

Expedia is a transactional online travel agency selling six verticals from one search interface: stays, flights, cars, packages, things to do, and cruises (High, live product). It sells to the general travelling public rather than a named segment, and the signed-out product does not ask who the traveller is before showing results (High, live product). Revenue mechanics visible on the surface include supplier compensation influencing accommodation ranking, paid listings labelled as ads, and a loyalty programme called One Key that issues OneKeyCash (Medium, first-party sort-order page; High for the ad labels and One Key prompts seen live). It operates desktop web, mobile web, and native apps, and promotes the app with a stated app-only discount (High, live product). Markets covered were not established in this session, and the US site is one of several country sites (Unknown for the full market list; the existence of a Spain site is High, seen in the site banner).

## 3. UX patterns

**Navigation model.** A persistent header carries a "Shop travel" menu, currency and country indicator, List your property, Support, Trips, an inbox icon, and Sign in. Beneath it, the search interface uses a horizontal vertical-picker (Stays, Flights, Cars, Packages, Things to do, Cruises) above a single search bar row. Flight results replace the picker with trip-type tabs (Roundtrip, One-way, Multi-city). Hotel results add a second tab row for property type (All stays, Hotels, Homes). Search state is carried in the URL, including the sort parameter. (High, live product.)

**Onboarding and first run.** No account, email or preference capture was requested before searching. Search runs signed out from the homepage. Sign-in is prompted repeatedly inside results rather than at entry, using benefit-framed copy: "Earn OneKeyCash on top of air miles when you sign in and book a flight", "Sign in for extra savings", "Get instant savings on this stay when you sign in and book". The auth wall sits at Trips, at the notifications and messages inbox, and at the Virtual Agent. (High, live product.)

**Primary flow, search to selection.** Enter destination and dates, land on results with a default sort, refine with a left-hand filter rail, open a property or fare, then reserve. Results pages present price context before choice: a seven-day price strip on flights, a price history statement, a price histogram on hotels, and a "Price is typical" statement on the property page. Filters are annotated with the price consequence of applying them, so the cost of each constraint is visible before it is selected. (High, live product.)

**Ranking transparency.** Both flight and hotel results carry a link to a single first-party page that sets out ranking factors per vertical. The page states that accommodation ranking takes into account how much Expedia is paid, including a partner programme that lets partners temporarily increase compensation for visibility, and states separately that flights are not ranked by compensation. It states that personalised savings are set by an algorithm using personal data including loyalty status, previous searches and booking history, and that changing device, browser or signing out may remove them. It also reserves the right to run limited tests that temporarily affect how listings are displayed, and to order results differently across apps and websites. This is policy-level disclosure at the results-page level. Per-result explanation was observed only in the generated highlights on the property page, which state a reason for each highlight. (Medium for the page, High for the link placement and the highlights.)

**Personalisation.** The default sort is labelled "Recommended" on flights and "Recommended for you" on hotels. Property pages generated a "Highlights for your solo trip" block matched to the single-traveller search parameter. Loyalty tier is stated as a condition for some savings. The basis for personalisation is described on the sort-order page rather than in the interface. (High for the interface labels, Medium for the stated basis.)

**AI surfaces.** Three distinct AI surfaces were observed, each narrow and each labelled. On the property page, a Beta-badged question box answers free-text questions about that property, attributes the answer to a source ("From the property"), states that it is an AI summary, carries an incorrectness disclaimer, and collects Yes or No feedback. Elsewhere on the property page, review-derived summaries appear inside amenity sections. In support, a Virtual Agent opens from a floating Help control and states on first contact: "Just to let you know, I sometimes use AI to answer your questions. Also, this conversation may be recorded to help improve your experience." Each surface is scoped to one task rather than presented as a general assistant. (High, live product.)

**Errors and empty states.** Two error states were observed. Unresolvable URLs return a full-page state headed "Sorry, it seems we've taken a wrong turn" offering Head Home, Find travel deals, and Sign in or register, retaining the header and a reduced footer. Auth-walled surfaces use a padlock illustration with an explanatory line and a single sign-in control, for example "Sign in to view notifications. View your notifications to stay updated on your trips." The signed-out Virtual Agent disables its text field and labels it "Typing is disabled". (High, live product.) No zero-results search state was reached.

**Notifications.** A price-change alert toggle exists on flight results and is off by default, which makes alerting opt-in at the point of search. The notifications inbox itself is behind sign-in, so notification content and cadence are `Unknown`. (High for the toggle and the wall, Unknown for content.)

**Reassurance mechanics.** Reassurance is presented as priced options and inline statements rather than a single named guarantee. Observed instances: the refundable-versus-non-refundable price delta per room, "Reserve now, pay later", "You will not be charged yet", the fees and city-tax block, the rate-restriction list, the trip protection prompt, "Total with taxes and fees" labelling, and the two all-in pricing messages. No single named protection scheme was found on the surfaces walked. That is absence of evidence from this session, not evidence of absence. (High for each observed instance.)

## 4. Notable design decisions

These look deliberate and are stated without evaluation.

1. **Filters are annotated with their price.** Each flight filter option displays the lowest price available under that constraint, including the flexibility filters. The cost of choosing a refundable fare or an included bag is legible before the filter is applied.
2. **Flexibility is a filter, not a sort.** Refundability and changeability can be filtered on in flights, but no sort option ranks by them. All nine flight sorts and all six hotel sorts resolve to price, time, distance, rating or star class.
3. **Ranking disclosure is linked from the results page itself, not only from the footer.** The link sits directly beneath the results heading in both verticals, adjacent to the sort control.
4. **The disclosure states that compensation influences accommodation ranking, and states that it does not influence flight ranking.** The asymmetry is written explicitly on the same page.
5. **All-in pricing is presented as a positioning claim.** A modal headed "We're not like the others. We include taxes and fees" and a toast reading "What you see is what you pay" both surface on hotel results, in addition to per-price "Total with taxes and fees" labels.
6. **AI is deployed as several narrow labelled surfaces rather than one assistant.** Property question answering, review summarisation and support each have their own entry point, their own label, and their own disclaimer. The property answer names its source and offers feedback controls.
7. **The AI disclosure appears before the user types.** The Virtual Agent states that it sometimes uses AI and that the conversation may be recorded as its second message, before any input is possible.
8. **Trip management is routed to the app.** The Trips web surface, signed out, presents an app-switch modal with a QR code and copy positioning the app as the place to view and manage a booked trip.
9. **Alerting is opt-in at the moment of search.** The price-change toggle sits inside the flight results rail and is off by default, so the alert is offered where the intent exists rather than at sign-up.
10. **Loyalty benefits are stated with their eligibility threshold on the page.** The VIP Access block names the tier required (Silver or above) rather than only naming the benefit.
11. **Rate restrictions are listed as plain statements on the rate.** One rate carried "You can't make changes, except for cancellations", "You can't use coupons", "You won't get VIP Access perks at the property".
