# Verified Competitor Profile · Hopper

**Phase:** 2 of 9, competitor 4 of 14
**Date researched:** 21 July 2026
**Researcher session conditions:** hopper.com and help.hopper.com, signed out, desktop web, browser located in Spain, English, USD, cookies denied at the consent banner.
**Platform covered:** desktop web and the public help centre. The mobile apps were not opened.
**Evidence supplied by the user:** none. Public sources only.

## Evidence note, read this first

Two things constrain this profile and both are recorded rather than worked around.

**The defining feature is app-exclusive.** Hopper's Price Prediction product page states "Exclusively available in the Hopper app" three separate times, directly beneath each of its three calls to action. The price-watch and prediction behaviour that Hopper is known for was therefore not observable on web, and no claim is made about how it looks or behaves.

**Flight results were not reachable.** A flight search was constructed successfully on the live site, including origin, destination and a date range, but submitting it cleared the airport fields and returned no results in this session. Several product pages also stopped at a "Just a moment" interstitial and did not render. These are recorded as access failures, not as evidence of absence.

No comparison to any other product appears in this document. That is Phase 3's job.

## 1. Snapshot

Hopper is a transactional travel platform selling four things from its web header: Stays, Flights, Cars, and a Deals surface (High, live product). Alongside inventory it sells a named set of paid ancillary services, listed in its own footer as Price Prediction, Change for Any Reason, Cancel for Any Reason, Leave for Any Reason, and Premium Disruption Assistance, with a Referral Program alongside them (High, footer). The help centre groups the ancillaries under the umbrella term "Flexible Travel Services and Trip Protection" and states plainly that none of them are insurance (Medium, help centre). Distribution is app-led: the homepage carries an app-download module citing store ratings and a scan-to-download code, and the signature prediction feature is stated as app-exclusive (High). The homepage also carries paid placement, with Sponsored labels on two homepage modules (High). Markets were not established; the popular-destinations list shown in this session was entirely United States cities, and prices displayed in USD despite the browser being located in Spain (High for what was displayed, Unknown for the market list).

## 3. UX patterns

**Two coexisting web experiences.** The root of hopper.com presents one interface: white wordmark, a four-tab vertical picker, a globe control, My Trips, and Sign In. A different interface renders at /flights/search: red wordmark, "Find Booking" in place of My Trips, an EN language control, a hamburger menu, and a persistent Help widget. Both render a four-band price calendar with the same legend values, but with different visual treatments, one pale and one saturated. Whether this is a live test, a partial migration, or two deliberately separate products was not established. (High for the observation, `Unknown` for the cause.)

**Price as the organising visual.** In both experiences the calendar itself carries the price information. Every date is a coloured chip, and the legend maps four colours to four price thresholds. Choosing dates is therefore a price decision before it is a schedule decision. Once a range is selected the colours are replaced by selection styling, so the band information for the chosen dates is no longer on screen. (High.)

**The signature feature is withheld from web.** The Price Prediction page markets the feature on the web and then routes every call to action to the app, stating the exclusivity three times on a single page. The web product sells inventory; the app carries the guidance layer. (High.)

**Ancillaries are a named product family with published boundaries.** The paid services are grouped under one term, listed together in the footer, and given their own help centre section. The help centre states what they are not, in plain words, before describing what they do: not insurance, not a substitute for insurance, benefits limited, buy insurance separately if you want protection. Each product links its own terms. (Medium.)

**Disruption recovery is written as a bounded promise.** The disruption article states a trigger threshold, a monetary cap, a cabin constraint, a time window, a usage limit, two alternative remedies, and three operational caveats including baggage and no-show risk. It also tells the traveller they may already be owed something by the airline. (Medium.)

**Booking lookup does not require an account.** The documented web route into the disruption product is a confirmation code plus a surname under My Trips. The second web experience labels this entry point "Find Booking" rather than "My Trips". (Medium for the route, High for the labelling.)

**Paid placement is labelled on the homepage.** Two homepage modules carry a visible "Sponsored" label above the offer. (High.)

**Consent.** The banner offers Deny and Accept All as two equally weighted controls, describes the tracking purpose as including tailored content and relevant ads, and is served by a third-party consent platform. (High.)

**Errors and empty states.** The help centre 404 states it cannot find the page, offers a route back to the FAQ home, and lists popular articles beneath. Several product pages did not progress past a "Just a moment" interstitial. The flight search form cleared its airport fields on submit rather than showing a validation message. (High.)

**Help centre.** Search-led, with a Popular Articles grid on the home page, three language options, breadcrumbs of the form Home, Hopper Products, Flexible Travel Services and Trip Protection, and a "Was this article helpful? YES NO" control on each article. Article titles alone evidence a wider product set than the footer lists, including Hopper Wallet, Savings Club, Hopper Trees, Travel Deal Tuesday and price matching. (High for the structure, Medium for what the titles evidence.)

## 4. Notable design decisions

These look deliberate and are stated without evaluation.

1. **The date picker is the price comparison tool.** Rather than presenting prices in a strip beside a conventional calendar, every date cell is itself a price signal, with a four-band legend.
2. **Four bands, not a gradient.** Price is quantised into four named thresholds rather than shown as a continuous scale.
3. **The price signal is removed once a choice is made.** Selection styling replaces the band colour on chosen dates.
4. **The guidance layer is reserved for the app while the transaction layer is on web.** Inventory can be browsed on the web; the recommendation about whether to buy cannot.
5. **The ancillary services are named as a family rather than sold as separate add-ons.** One umbrella term, one help centre section, one shared disclaimer.
6. **The disclaimer leads with what the product is not.** The insurance article answers "No" as its first word, then explains, then recommends buying actual insurance.
7. **Each ancillary links its own terms and conditions from the shared article.**
8. **The disruption remedy offers a choice rather than a single outcome.** Rebook, or take a full refund and keep the original reservation.
9. **The disruption promise is capped and constrained in public.** A dollar cap per traveller, a same-cabin rule, a same-day window, and one use per booking, all stated on the help page rather than only in terms.
10. **The help article tells the traveller they may already have rights against the airline**, alongside selling them the paid remedy.
11. **Recovery is reachable without an account**, through a confirmation code and surname.
12. **Both self-service and a phone agent are offered for the same disruption flow.**
13. **Sponsored placements are labelled on the homepage** above the offer rather than beside it.
14. **Consent presents Deny with equal visual weight to Accept All.**
15. **Marketing reproduces App Store reviews as the evidence for prediction quality**, rather than publishing a measured accuracy figure.
