# Verified Competitor Profile · Tripadvisor

**Phase:** 2 of 9, competitor 6 of 14
**Date researched:** 21 July 2026
**Researcher session conditions:** tripadvisor.com, signed out, desktop web, browser located in Spain, English, USD prices shown.
**Platform covered:** desktop web only. The mobile apps were not opened.
**Evidence supplied by the user:** none. Public sources only.

## Scope note

Per the benchmark plan, Tripadvisor is in scope for two specific things: trust signals and social proof at the compare stage, and how conversational planning is presented to someone with no frame of reference. This profile weights those two areas and treats the transactional plumbing more lightly. Restaurants and Cruises are top-level verticals but were not walked.

One observation carries a caveat, flagged where it appears: the AI Assistant panel was already open and populated with a first-time-visitor Lisbon plan when the homepage loaded. The output was rendered live in the product and is recorded as observed, but I did not type the originating prompt myself, so I cannot claim to have driven that flow from a clean start.

No comparison to any other product appears in this document. That is Phase 3's job.

## 1. Snapshot

Tripadvisor is a review and discovery platform that monetises through experience bookings, advertising and referrals rather than by owning inventory. Its own homepage describes it as backed by "more than a billion reviews" (High, live product). The core unit everywhere is the bubble rating, a score out of 5 shown as filled bubbles, paired with a review count (High). Experiences and attractions are bookable, but the booking is fulfilled by Viator, described on the page as "a Tripadvisor company" (High). Revenue surfaces visible in this session include a labelled Sponsored carousel on hotels, "from $X per adult" experience pricing, a Tripadvisor Rewards programme offering "$30 off Things to Do", and first-party documentation of a Sponsored Placements advertising product (High for the on-page items, Medium for the ad product). A conversational planner branded "Plan with AI" runs alongside the site. Markets and currency: an English interface with USD prices was served from a browser located in Spain (High for the observation, Unknown for the rule).

## 3. UX patterns

**Navigation model.** A persistent header carries the wordmark, a "Plan with AI" entry, a saved-items control and Sign in. Beneath the homepage hero sits a single search with five vertical tabs. Destination pages use a readable breadcrumb, for example Europe, Portugal, Central Portugal, Lisbon District, Lisbon, Things to Do in Lisbon. (High.)

**The bubble rating is the atomic unit.** Everything is scored on the same 5-bubble scale and everything shows the count of reviews behind that score. The count is never hidden, so a 5.0 from 8 reviews and a 4.5 from 41,083 reviews are visually distinguishable at a glance. Sample size travels with the score. (High.)

**Ranking is explained on the page.** The attractions ranking prints its basis in one sentence, naming traveler reviews, ratings, page views and user location, and offers a further "Learn how recommendations are selected" link. Explanation sits inline with the ranked list rather than in a footer. (High.)

**Social proof is layered, and it includes a named human.** Three distinct trust layers appear on one destination page: the aggregate bubble score with its review count, a stream of individual attributed reviews with travel-party context, and a single named Travel Expert mini-guide with a stated personal credential. The expert layer is editorial and human, and it speaks directly to a first-timer's anxieties, hills, footwear, queue timing, rather than to price. (High.)

**Reviews are shown warts and all.** The public review stream interleaves strong praise and blunt complaints. Negative reviews are not filtered out of the visible stream, and each carries the same structural metadata as positive ones. (High.)

**Conversational planning is a running assistant, not a one-shot answer.** The "Plan with AI" panel produced a first-timer Lisbon itinerary organised as one neighbourhood per day, opened with a plain-language logistics tip naming the local transit card ("pick up a Viva Viagem card and load it as you go, this keeps boarding fast and avoids ticket hassles"), and linked to official transit sources. It then asked two clarifying follow-ups rather than stopping: a pace question ("Relaxed / Balanced / Fast, see-everything") and a scope question ("Do you want to day-trip to Sintra? Yes / No"), each as tappable chips. An AI disclaimer sat at the top of the panel: "This assistant is powered by AI. Responses may not always be accurate. Don't share personal data," with a Learn more link. The panel attributed the session to context with "Asked while viewing Home", and offered New chat, Share and Close controls. (High for the rendered content, subject to the scope-note caveat that I did not originate the prompt.)

**The planner reduces stress in its own words.** The itinerary framing was explicitly newcomer-oriented: a heading "3 easy days in Lisbon (first-timer, simple logistics)", a stated principle of "one area per day so you're not crisscrossing the city", and a base recommendation chosen for walkability and transit. The pace question offers a low-effort option first. This is planning organised around effort and confidence, not around price. (High, subject to the same caveat.)

**Booking is a handoff, and the handoff partner is named.** Experiences are bookable, but the fulfilment is Viator, and the page states the relationship plainly. Scarcity cues such as "Likely to sell out" are attributed to Viator's booking data and a 30-day window rather than presented as Tripadvisor's own claim. (High.)

**Advertising is labelled and grouped.** Sponsored hotel inventory appears in a labelled carousel rather than interleaved into organic results. A separate Sponsored Placements product exists for owners. (High for the on-page label, Medium for the product.)

**Discovery is offered on non-price axes.** The homepage groups things to do by interest (Outdoors, Food, Culture, Water) and the destination page offers roughly 55 counted categories. Neither entry point is price-led. (High.)

**Consent.** No cookie consent modal was encountered in this session on the surfaces walked. `Unknown` whether this reflects prior consent state or its absence. (Recorded as not observed.)

**Errors and empty states.** One error state was observed: an unrecognised path returned a themed 404, "Meet the 404th wonder of the world", with links to Hotels, Restaurants and Things to Do. (High.)

**Accessibility.** No accessibility statement was sought or found in this session. Attractions carried a "temporarily closed" status label and experiences carried language labels, but neither is an accessibility signal. `Unknown`. (Recorded as not sought.)

## 4. Notable design decisions

These look deliberate and are stated without evaluation.

1. **Sample size is never separated from the score.** Every bubble rating is shown with its review count, so the confidence behind a score is always legible.
2. **The ranking basis is printed next to the ranking**, naming four factors including page views and user location.
3. **Trust is delivered in three layers on one page**: an aggregate score, an attributed individual-review stream, and a single named human expert guide.
4. **The expert guide is a person with a stated stake in the place**, not an anonymous editor, and the credential is shown.
5. **The expert speaks to newcomer anxiety directly**, addressing terrain, footwear and queue timing rather than price or availability.
6. **Negative reviews stay in the visible stream** alongside positive ones, with identical structure.
7. **Every review carries travel-party context** (solo, family, couple, friends), so a reader can weight relevance to their own situation.
8. **The conversational planner asks before it assumes.** It follows an itinerary with a pace question and a scope question rather than committing to one plan.
9. **The planner leads its options with the lowest-effort choice** (Relaxed first, "No, stay in Lisbon" as an option).
10. **The planner opens with a logistics reassurance**, naming the local transit card and how to avoid ticket friction, and cites official sources.
11. **The AI disclaimer is placed at the top of the assistant**, before its output, and warns against sharing personal data.
12. **The booking handoff partner is named on the page**, and scarcity claims are attributed to that partner's data rather than asserted directly.
13. **Advertising is labelled and grouped** rather than interleaved.
14. **Discovery is organised by interest and category, not by price.**
15. **Reviewing, ranking and rewards are tied into one loop**: users write reviews, reviews decide the Travelers' Choice awards, and a rewards programme pays users to plan, book and review.
