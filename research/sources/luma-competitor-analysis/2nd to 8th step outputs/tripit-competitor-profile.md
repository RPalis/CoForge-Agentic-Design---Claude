# Verified Competitor Profile · TripIt

**Phase:** 2 of 9, competitor 3 of 14
**Date researched:** 21 July 2026
**Researcher session conditions:** tripit.com and help.tripit.com, signed out, desktop web, browser located in Spain, English, cookies rejected at the consent modal.
**Platform covered:** the public web site and the public help centre only.
**Evidence supplied by the user:** none. Public sources only.

## Evidence warning, read this first

**The confidence ceiling on this profile is lower than on the previous two, and the reason is structural.** TripIt has no browsable product. Every itinerary surface sits behind account creation, and the features that define the product run in the mobile app rather than on the web. Creating an account with real personal details is outside the walkthrough limits, so no itinerary, no alert, no map and no notification was seen in operation.

What this means in practice: the observations below are almost entirely **Tier 2 first-party documentation**, principally the public help centre, which is unusually detailed and carries per-article modification dates. Very little is **Tier 1 hands-on**. Where the help centre describes how a screen behaves, that is documentation of intended behaviour, not an observation of it. It is tagged Medium throughout and should not be read as though a screen was seen.

No comparison to any other product appears in this document. That is Phase 3's job.

## 1. Snapshot

TripIt is an itinerary organiser. It sells no travel inventory. The user books anywhere, then forwards the confirmation email to plans@tripit.com or authorises inbox scanning, and the product assembles a single itinerary per trip (Medium, help centre and product pages). It runs as a mobile app with a companion web site, and states availability on phone, watch, desktop and Mac (Medium, product pages). Revenue is a subscription: the free tier is free, and TripIt Pro is stated at $49 per year with a 30-day free trial (Medium, pricing page). A separate documented customer segment exists through SAP Concur, with its own help centre category covering activation and travel arrangers (Medium, help centre). Markets and non-USD pricing were not established this session (Unknown). Positioning language on the homepage aims at frequent and business travellers, with testimonials referencing 100 days a year on the road and business travel (Low, marketing copy).

## 3. UX patterns

**Navigation model, public web.** A five-item marketing navigation: TripIt, TripIt Pro, How It Works, Pricing, Sign In, plus a distinct "Sign Up, It's Free" call to action styled as the primary control. The free and Pro propositions have separate pages under /web/free and /web/pro, and the pricing page sits under /web/pro/pricing. (High, live public site.)

**Onboarding and first run.** The stated first action is not account creation but forwarding an email. The How It Works page presents three numbered steps: forward your confirmation emails, get a comprehensive itinerary, download the app. Step one exposes the address plans@tripit.com with a copy control that confirms "Copied to clipboard" on use. The signed-out path to the product itself is a conventional email and password sign-in with third-party options, a "Keep me signed in" checkbox and password recovery. (High for the page and the sign-in form, Medium for the described sequence.)

**Capture model.** Three documented paths, described in order of automation: forward an email, authorise inbox scanning, or enter manually. Capture is documented as supplier-agnostic across thousands of vendors in five languages, and the published sample list spans airlines, hotels, car rental, rail and bus, cruise, parking, agencies, activities and restaurants. Confirmation of capture is documented as a return email that states whether a new trip was created or a plan was added to an existing one. (Medium.)

**Personalisation.** Two documented personalisation mechanisms are notable because neither is based on price or purchase history. Travel Guidance derives its answers from the nationality on a stored passport, the home city in the profile, and the previous country on the itinerary. Personal Risk Level asks the user to declare a risk tolerance, then flags plans whose nighttime risk exceeds it. Both are declared or profile-derived rather than inferred from behaviour. (Medium.)

**Notifications and proactive help.** Alerts are documented as push notifications with in-app counterparts: an Alerts tab, indicators beside the affected plan in the trip timeline, and indicators in flight details. Control is documented at two levels, a global toggle under Push Notifications and per-alert dismissal scoped either to one plan or to all plans in a trip. Go Now is documented as a countdown that begins up to 3 hours 55 minutes ahead with a push 10 minutes before the leave time, and it is stated to require Always Allow and Precise Location. (Medium.)

**Free and paid boundary.** The pricing page presents the boundary as two parallel 16-item lists, with Pro framed as "Everything in TripIt (free), plus". The split places organisation, static maps, safety scores, transport options, nearby places, compensation eligibility, carbon tracking, sharing and calendar sync in the free tier, and places real-time alerting, in-airport navigation, disruption monitoring, country entry guidance and reward tracking in Pro. One feature appears on both sides at different capacities: document upload, at 3 per trip free and 25 per trip on Pro. (Medium.)

**Help centre.** Eight categories with visible article counts totalling 142: Updates and Support 9, Account Email Settings 22, TripIt Pro 30, Travel Itinerary 38, TripIt Apps 25, Inbox Sync 7, SAP Concur Customers 5, Calendar 6. Every article carries a "Modified on" date, a "Was this article helpful? No / Yes" control, a Print control, sibling links under "Articles in this folder" and lateral links under "You may like to read". Articles name third-party data partners explicitly, and one article carried today's date. (High, observed.)

**Errors and empty states.** One error state was observed. A non-existent path returns a themed page reading "Sorry, this page has been canceled. Re-route your destination here", with a link to the help centre. The former help centre host returns a third-party 404 stating the instance is no longer available, which is that vendor's page rather than TripIt's. (High.)

**Consent.** The cookie modal is served by a third-party consent platform and offers Accept All Cookies, Reject All, and a separate link to view and change preferences. (High.)

**Accessibility.** No accessibility statement was found on the public site or in the help centre this session. `Unknown`. This is absence of evidence from one session and does not establish that none exists.

## 4. Notable design decisions

These look deliberate and are stated without evaluation.

1. **The product has no search.** There is nothing to browse, compare or rank. The documented entry point to the entire product is an email address.
2. **The first action requires no account.** The published sequence puts forwarding an email before downloading the app, and the address is presented with a copy control on a public page.
3. **Safety is a first-class, structured data type rather than editorial content.** A 1 to 100 scale, a composite plus separate day and night scores, six named sub-categories, and a published methodology with a named partner and named upstream sources.
4. **Safety sub-categories name specific populations.** Women's safety and LGBTQ safety are separate scored dimensions with stated components, alongside health, physical harm, basic freedoms and theft.
5. **Risk tolerance is a user setting, not a system judgement.** Personal Risk Level asks the traveller to declare their own threshold across five levels, and the product then flags plans against that declared threshold rather than against a single global standard.
6. **Safety scores attach to specific plan types rather than to a city.** Lodging, rental car, restaurant, train station, ferry terminal, shuttle and activity locations are each scored.
7. **In-airport navigation is treated as a mapping problem, not a signage problem.** Step-by-step walking directions, walking-time estimates between any two arbitrary points, and searchable interior points including restrooms, ATMs and charging stations.
8. **Airport map coverage is published as a finite named list.** Roughly 110 airports are enumerated by name and code, and the article states explicitly what the user sees when a map is not available.
9. **Interactive maps are stated to work offline after first load**, for the remainder of the trip.
10. **Departure timing is expressed as a countdown with a specific horizon.** Up to 3 hours 55 minutes, with a push 10 minutes before the leave time, rather than an open-ended reminder.
11. **Disruption alerts are dismissible at two scopes**, one plan or all plans in the trip.
12. **Entry requirements are answerable before a trip exists.** Travel Guidance is documented as searchable by country, dates and nationality from the Pro tab.
13. **Multiple passports are a supported case**, with nationality read from the stored passport rather than assumed from the home country.
14. **Third-party data providers are named in help articles**, specifically GeoSure, Riskline and Basetrip.
15. **The free and paid split is published as two parallel lists of equal length**, with the paid tier framed additively.
16. **Post-trip value is non-transactional.** Compensation eligibility, in-flight ratings, travel statistics and carbon tracking sit after the trip, and none of them sells anything.
17. **The error page is written in the product's own idiom**, using flight-cancellation and re-routing language.

**The ratio matters more than the total.** Twelve High claims against forty-nine Medium is a materially weaker evidence base than the two profiles before it, and the weakness is not fixable by more desk work. Reaching Tier 1 on this competitor requires an account and a device, and both sit outside the current limits. Phase 3 should carry this asymmetry forward rather than presenting TripIt cells in the matrix as though they were observed at the same tier as the others.
